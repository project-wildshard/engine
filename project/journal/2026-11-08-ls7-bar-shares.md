# LS7: pipelined first-frame fences and measured bar shares

> Written by: Claude

The Load bar ignored row weights: `loadingFraction` gave every row an equal share, so the first frame's 7 s of real GPU work got 1/20 of the bar, the same as a 0.1 s row, and d9c38da's weight change from 3 to 4 changed nothing. Rows now carry their path weight, and the bar weighs them. The weights a shardfile boot runs are its measured seconds: Sunscar Dunes, phone tier, the iPhone 16 Pro profile at 4x CPU in headless Chromium, game-stage spans from `loading-card-proof.mjs --throttle --dump`. Renderer 0.14 s gives 0.1. Weapon, menu and audio measured 0.00 s and get 0.01. Shaders 0.59 s gives 0.6, first frame 6.67 s gives 6.7, and Server world 1.86 s gives 1.9. The sources are written next to each number in `steps.ts` and `loadingPaths.ts`. Server's old 0.01 share hid its 48 real steps once weights counted, which left a 1.86 s window.

The first frame's fenced units are pipelined. The next unit is submitted before the last fence resolves, at most two in flight, and each unit counts when its own fence signals. This saved only 0.2 to 0.4 s. The units are bound by SwiftShader's pipeline builds, not idle frames. Measured in the same session on the same machine, first frame takes 7.03 s at f65485e (no fences, ready 22.1 s), 6.84 s at d9c38da (ready 21.8 s) and 6.3 to 7.2 s now (ready 21.3 to 22.4 s). The 19.5 s handoff figure came from a quieter machine. Strict proof: all four runs pass. The worst 1 % window is 1.56 s on Dunes in Chromium, 1.13 s on the template in Chromium, and under 1 s in WebKit.
