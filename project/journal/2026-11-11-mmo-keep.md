# What a player keeps (MMO-PLAN MP3.1, MP3.3, J202's world-state rule)

- `test/dedicated-keep.test.ts` proves three Friends-grid rows on the real Driftwood and Nalati rooms, with Postgres (PGlite):
  a restart gives a fresh world (the creature that died is back at full health, the tick starts over) and the player keeps
  their coins; coins earned in Driftwood are absent in Nalati and there on return (bags are `(player, shard)` rows); and 100
  random kills, with restarts falling among them on a seeded die, pay no fact twice: every receipt is one row, no ledger line
  is heard twice, and the purse is exactly the sum of the coin receipts.
- Nothing in `src/` changed: E7.4's ledger, boot ids and per-shard docs already hold. The rows needed their proof.

## MP1.2: the replica on the box

- `mmo-bots.mjs replica --remote <ssh host> [--cpus 9-13]`: the staging release's bundle runs on the box as the ssh user,
  pinned to staging's cores, on its loopback, and an ssh tunnel brings it to this Mac, where the bots run (J202) with their
  forwarded addresses as on a local replica. Smoke, 4 bots in Nalati: p95 mean 7.8 ms, worst 9.7 ms, 59.9 ticks/s.
- The first cleanup's `pkill -f` matched its own shell and the directory stayed: the pattern now starts `[/]`.
- Codex's review of `dedicated-keep.test.ts` asked for 4 MUSTs, folded by opus-mp3:
  1. J202 restarts through `room.drain()`, the write queue and Postgres, and the player is admitted by `saves.read()`.
  2. Each restart in the 100 kills takes one of five orderings on a seeded die: a clean drain, a crash after the last checkpoint with
     one live grant lost, a commit that fails, a commit whose answer is lost, and a torn newest doc that boots from `previous` with
     its recovery receipts. Old save writes are replayed after every drain, and some corpses are hit again.
  3. Exactly once is checked against an oracle: every `reward.granted` the game emitted, by boot, has one receipt if its process
     committed and none if a crash lost it, and every kill counter's fact counts 1..n once each.
  4. MP3.3 runs two players through the real door, whose `where()` picks the cell from the place row: four bags, each there on return.
- Found and fixed: a filing whose id the player's book already held (a replayed tick, a boot id that comes round again) still
  reached the purse, so the purse paid twice while the book kept one receipt. `DedicatedRoom` now files only what the book lacks
  (`LedgerBook.has`). A test that replays one boot proves it, and fails without the fix.
