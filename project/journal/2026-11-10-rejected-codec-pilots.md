# Keep the original snapshot codec after two measured pilots

The actual earned Nalati hostile-client save contains 22,118,549 native
physics bytes and 15,805,161 literal/reference bytes. The synchronous
writer profile identifies reference encoding and compression, rather
than base64 packing, as the material work. This is a measured profile,
not a reason to weaken the original wire serialization/restore oracle.

A private zero-word dictionary pilot retained complete encoded and
reconstructed byte equality, but its warmed complete-case driver walls
were 5.183 and 5.433 seconds versus the last original control at 4.949.
The first control was cold at 9.780 seconds and is not a valid warmed
comparison. Probe-order changes were inconclusive too.

A second private pilot passed the identical remaining-byte bound into
matching. Its isolated warm encoder improved, yet eight actual original
Nalati hostile-client cases gave median case times 3,493.072 ms original
versus 3,757.355 ms candidate. Every original ten-second case deadline,
full wire save, strict restore and gameplay assertion stayed intact.
The earned snapshot's reference and decoded bytes matched in every
isolated round: 52 pairs in the first investigation and 32 in the second.

Neither candidate was retained. The original codec, serialization
oracle, clocks and limits stay unchanged, with no saving credited.
A future change needs a whole-case benefit as well as exact bytes.
