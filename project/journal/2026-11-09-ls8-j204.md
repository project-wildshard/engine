# LS8 under J204: the bar never freezes for 2 s

> Written by: Claude

J204 (Jake, 2026-11-09) amends J188: "the bar just has to move, it can move by 0.1% ... the whole point is purely that
download or loading can't freeze for >2s." Weigh steps by measured cost per kind, and prove on a real GPU too.

- The rule (`scripts/loading-bar-rule.mjs`) counts a visible tenth of a percent, within float error, in every 2 s. The
  card's Load number shows tenths, so 91.2 to 91.3 is visible; `data-setup` and the timeline keep whole percents.
- `loading-card-proof.mjs --gpu`: Chromium on this Mac's Metal GPU, with `--throttle`'s 4x CPU.
- Sky: Driftwood's backdrop spends 1.5 s in the engine's `clientMaterials` (the KTX2 transcoder and the graph
  compiler), which now count each texture and the compiler on the sky step through `lookGraphMaterials`.
- Props: the shardfile world hook (`ShardfileClient.world`) counts its ten sub-steps on its step.
- Ready frame: the spawn view, minimap and horizon count as they finish.
- First frame: each unit weighs its kind's measured seconds (drawable 0.158, shadow map 0.152, view 0.192, post pass 0.158
  on the calibration profile). A shaders step slower than 15 ms a program (SwiftShader 34, Metal 7, WebKit 2.4) starts
  the first frame narrow, one new material and one new layout a slice, without widening: the slow-fence fallback came a
  slice late, and widening put the view-model swords and arms, three layouts of one material, in one 2.3 s frame.

Two tries measured and dropped: drawing the first shadow maps in caster groups (2.7 s, then 6.5 s a group) and a narrow
probe that widened after three fast slices (the cheap sky slices come first).

Driftwood, strict, before (`7c81cb7`) and after: Chromium GPU 4x 1 stall (sky 2.4 s) to 0, ready 28 s, first frame 1.0 to
0.95 s; WebKit 0 to 0, first frame 0.72 s both; SwiftShader 4x 5 stalls (2.0-2.4 s) to 2 at the edge (first shadow map
2.0 s, ready jobs 2.1 s) while a CPU soak held two slots.
